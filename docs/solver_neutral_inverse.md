# Solver-neutral implicit inverse baseline

> **Status: implemented and validated on 2026-09-02; extended to a lobed
> star target on 2026-09-03. The canonical-curve/MLP path remains
> experimental: its radial-Fourier/continuation correction now recovers the
> canonical star essentially exactly, while the distilled MLP still misses its
> separate `0.2 mm` representation-convergence gate.**
> This page describes the low-dimensional
> frequency-domain inverse in `solvers/sdf_inverse/` and its MOD/Kress
> comparison driver. It does not replace the separate MOD-only neural B-scan
> adjoint.

The 2026-09-05 [first-batch implementation](sdf_kress_first_batch_2026-09-05.md)
adds opt-in `curve_only` / `export_only` policies, separate reconstruction and
representation statuses, and smooth continuous-curve distance targets. The
alternating-loop description below remains the unchanged `legacy_strict`
default. The new ablation reproduces the saved canonical star with identical
coefficients but no SDF queries during curve-only reconstruction; one-shot
export still fails its fixed training budget. See that report for measured
timings, compatibility mapping and independent export tolerances.

The separate [2026-09-06 follow-up](sdf_kress_followup_2026-09-06.md) adds
verified discrete Kress geometry/material directional derivatives and a
paired-objective adjoint. These are opt-in; the comparison and alternating
drivers described below retain their numerical Jacobians and defaults.
The follow-up's separate `run_neural_metric_comparison.py` uses frozen
initial neural-feature covariances on the canonical curve, while
`run_material_inverse_comparison.py` estimates one bounded interior
permittivity with fixed or joint radial-K2 geometry. Neither is a direct
trained/re-extracted SDF inverse or a multiple-material/topology extension.

The [explicit material robustness extension](material_robustness_2026-09-06.md)
adds a separate `run_material_robustness_comparison.py` and
`sdf_inverse.robust_material_inverse` API: bounded training-only restarts and
frequency continuation, with final selection on a common full-band objective.
It preserves the D chart/bounds and all baseline defaults; no SDF, additional
material region, or target/held-out selection is introduced.

## What is now working

[`run_sdf_inverse_comparison.py`](../run_sdf_inverse_comparison.py) can start
from a deliberately wrong circle SDF

```text
center = (0.48, 0.52) m, radius = 0.065 m
```

and recovers the analytic target

```text
center = (0.50, 0.50) m, radius = 0.050 m.
```

The observations are complex scattered fields from the independent analytic
penetrable-cylinder Mie series in `gpr_bem_ref`, not data generated by either
solver under test. The star target described below replaces that series with
the independent `nystrom_ref` oracle. Within either target case, MOD and Kress
use the same implicit field, the same fitted ordered
curve at every objective evaluation, the same normalized frequency-domain
residual, the same parameter finite differences, the same bounds, and the
same damped Gauss--Newton/Levenberg--Marquardt policy. Only the forward-solver
dispatch changes.

This gives a small, auditable answer to two questions: can an incorrect implicit field
converge under the complete extraction/remeshing/forward pipeline, and how
does forward-solver error affect that recovery? It is intentionally not a
scalable neural inverse.

## Pipeline and ownership

```text
Torch implicit model (distance property optional)
  -> TorchImplicitField2D, negative inside
  -> marching-squares extraction and safeguarded level-set projection
  -> exactly one validated closed component
  -> Method-B Fourier fit and arc-length redistribution
  -> immutable even-node PeriodicCurve2D
       |                         |
       | unchanged curve        | copied points, normals, arc weights
       v                         v
  gpr_bem_kress             MOD boundary adapter
  full source x receiver    ImplicitBoundarySamples2D
  field -> paired diagonal  -> paired MOD field
       |                         |
       +------------+------------+
                    v
       normalized complex scattered-field residual
                    v
       bounded parameter finite differences + damped Gauss--Newton
```

The implementation is separated by responsibility:

- `solvers/sdf_inverse/geometry.py` performs the common SDF extraction,
  single-component validation, Method-B fit, and discretization. Its MOD
  adapter changes only the container and copies the ordered curve's nodes,
  normals, and periodic arc-length weights; it does not rebuild or compress a
  second geometry.
- `solvers/sdf_inverse/forward.py` owns the solver-neutral paired experiment
  and dispatch. MOD runs strict quadrature, direct unsquared Müller solve,
  analytic-extrapolated normal derivatives, NumPy, and complex128. Kress runs
  its native `PeriodicCurve2D` solve and explicitly selects the diagonal of
  its full source-by-receiver response.
- `solvers/sdf_inverse/models.py` supplies the exact three-parameter circle
  SDF, a rotated quadratic ellipse level set, a seeded topology-constrained
  random-feature neural implicit, and bounded Torch parameter controllers.
  The controller refuses an unexpectedly large parameter set rather than
  making full-network finite differences look practical.
- `solvers/sdf_inverse/targets.py` owns the analytic star: one object supplies
  the periodic parameterization the oracle integrates, the Torch implicit
  field the extraction pipeline consumes, and the exact point-to-curve
  distance the geometry gates use, so those three cannot drift apart.
- `solvers/sdf_inverse/nystrom_oracle.py` wraps `nystrom_ref` as an
  observation source: complex wavenumbers from the shared material
  definition, a unit-strength solve scaled by the problem's per-frequency
  source strength, the paired diagonal of the full response, and a
  half-resolution self-convergence check the driver gates on.
- `solvers/sdf_inverse/optimization.py` owns the fixed complex-data objective,
  numerical Jacobian, damping, bound projection, backtracking, evaluation
  cache, accepted-iterate history, and explicit stop reason.

The residual normalizes each complex frequency column by the norm of the
observed column, applies optional frequency weights, and concatenates real and
imaginary parts for real least squares. This definition remains nonzero and
well-defined for one frequency; it does not use a one-point trapezoidal
frequency integral.

## Alternating MLP-SDF inverse

[`run_mlp_sdf_inverse_comparison.py`](../run_mlp_sdf_inverse_comparison.py) is
the solver-neutral neural successor to the analytic-family baseline. It is a
separate driver because a useful MLP has thousands of weights and cannot be
dropped into the parameter-by-parameter finite-difference optimizer.

The accepted optimization geometry is a validated `PeriodicCurve2D`. A smooth
coordinate MLP with a fixed wrong-circle residual skip is trained as the
signed-distance representation of that contour. The skip is a geometric
initialization, not a shape constraint: all MLP layers are trainable and can
move the represented zero set away from a circle. Its last layer starts at
zero, so random hidden weights begin with exactly one valid closed contour
instead of an arbitrary collection of open or missing zero sets. Fixed
multiscale Fourier input features reduce the usual spectral bias when fitting
lobes. The model still reports `claims_signed_distance = False`; finite-sample
training cannot prove the Eikonal equation globally.

Separating these two roles is deliberate. The MLP's extracted contour is an
audited representation, not the state used to build the next numerical
Jacobian. This prevents grid extraction, Fourier-fit, and re-distancing error
from becoming a persistent geometry update.

One outer cycle is transactional:

```text
canonical ordered contour and its accepted BEM response
  -> central finite differences in its gauge-fixed radial Fourier state
  -> cheap solver-node validation and direct MOD/Kress solves for the probes
  -> damped Gauss--Newton step, Armijo backtracking, and direct data decrease
  -> densely validate the identical selected radial-state curve
  -> train every MLP weight against that curve's negative-inside polygon distance
     + zero-contour anchors + normal offsets + Eikonal loss
  -> extract the trained MLP zero set once and enforce the representation-drift gate
  -> accept the radial state and its already-audited probe response as the next state
```

The controls are mean radius, exact Cartesian centre translation, and
`cos(k t), sin(k t)` radius coefficients for `k=2..maximum_mode`. Radial mode
one is gauge-fixed to zero because exact centre translation supplies those two
degrees of freedom. The initial contour is projected into this chart once;
accepted states update the coefficients without refitting, which both enforces
star-shapedness and prevents spectral leakage above the declared mode. The
coordinates include an unknown lobe component without hard-coding that the
answer is a star. A larger MLP therefore
does not increase BEM count: one central Jacobian needs nominally `2(2k+1)`
direct curve forwards plus line-search trials. Re-distancing performs no
additional production BEM solve. It is still an expensive black-box inverse,
but unlike weight-wise finite differences its forward cost is independent of
network width, and unlike the earlier implementation it does not repeat dense
SDF boundary extraction inside every probe.

`--maximum-mode` defaults to the smaller of two limits. The wave limit is
`ceil(ka + ka**(1/3))`, evaluated on the training band and initial mean radius.
The scan-angle limit is `floor((N_angles - 1)/2)`, because an even `N`-angle
ring has only an unpaired cosine at its Nyquist mode and cannot identify both
real sine/cosine coefficients there. In particular, the 12-pair star scan used
to select `k=7` from `ka` even though modes above `k=5` alias on that
acquisition. The full resolved benchmark selects `k=5`. An explicit larger value
remains allowed, but `metrics.json` reports the separate wave and angle limits
and which one the request exceeds.

The default is cumulative low-to-high frequency continuation. For the standard
0.5/1.5/2.5 GHz, `k<=5` run it activates 0.5 GHz/K3, then
0.5+1.5 GHz/K5, then the full band/K5. This matters because measured initial
directions from the ellipse start are target-aligned at 0.5 GHz/K3 and
target-misaligned for the joint full-band objective. `--joint-full-band` (and
its compatibility aliases) retains the latter as an explicit ablation.

Two alternative selectors remain for controlled experiments.
`--progressive-mode-continuation` keeps the full frequency band active while
the ceiling grows through `k=1/3/5`; it resets damping between stages and
carries unused accepted-update budget into the final stage.
`--frequency-continuation` explicitly selects the default cumulative schedule.
Stage metadata records the chosen strategy. Losses are
comparable across progressive-mode stages because their objective is fixed;
frequency-continuation trajectories require stage-local loss segments.

### Completed cumulative-frequency diagnostic and the first correction

The completed Kress bundle
[`mlp-canonical-ellipse-to-star-kress-20260903/`](../results/inverse_solver_comparison/mlp-canonical-ellipse-to-star-kress-20260903/)
is useful failure evidence, not a convergence result. It accepted 15 updates,
then every continuation stage stopped with
`no_acceptable_mlp_distillation`. Full-band loss changed
`2.223592919 -> 2.157108810`, training relative L2
`1.194114882 -> 1.031121445`, and holdout relative L2
`1.003360055 -> 0.942955819`. The canonical maximum boundary error did improve
from `42.0309` to `27.8343 mm`, but the shape remained far from the target.
The final MLP-to-canonical maximum drift was `0.497114 mm` and its relative
response discrepancy was `0.00406356`.

The cumulative first stage explained the misleading partial progress. Its
low-frequency objective drove shrinkage and translation before the lobe was
observable: mean radius moved from `50.982` to `40.345 mm`. The final `k=5`
component had the wrong phase, and its vector error worsened from
`12.480` to `14.446 mm`. The intermediate correction therefore exposed only
the mode basis progressively while keeping all training frequencies in the
data term. It motivated the next diagnostic; it did not establish that
progressive mode exposure would converge.

### Completed progressive-mode diagnostic and the joint correction

The subsequent Kress bundle
[`mlp-full-band-modes-ellipse-to-star-kress-20260903/`](../results/inverse_solver_comparison/mlp-full-band-modes-ellipse-to-star-kress-20260903/)
also did not converge. It accepted 74 updates. Training loss improved
`2.223592919 -> 1.268193860` and training relative L2 improved
`1.194115 -> 0.957988`, but holdout relative L2 worsened
`1.003360 -> 1.075114` and canonical maximum boundary error worsened
`42.031 -> 48.442 mm`.

Intrinsic geometry confirms that this was a wrong basin rather than benign
reparameterization. The final centre error was `29.206 mm`, mean radius was
`44.577 mm`, and the residual ellipse harmonic had amplitude `11.652 mm`.
The `k=5` vector was `(-3.849, -1.253) mm`, amplitude `4.048 mm`: nearly
inverted relative to the target, with `16.397 mm` vector error. Dense target
distance had maximum/RMS `49.135/19.457 mm`.

The neural representation was faithful to that poor canonical solution:
maximum drift was `0.243 mm`, response discrepancy was `0.002329` relative,
and none of the 74 re-distancing attempts failed the MLP fit, extraction, or
drift gates. Stage 1 accepted 20 `k=1` updates; stage 2 accepted four `k<=3`
updates and recorded 37 dense-validation rejections; stage 3 then accepted 50
`k<=5` updates. Delaying the lobe-carrying mode until after 24 accepted
low-order updates is consistent with entry into the bad basin, but the
historical comparison does not uniquely establish causality.

There is positive historical evidence for exposing the full basis from the
start. In
[`mlp-ellipse-to-star-nystrom-20260903/`](../results/inverse_solver_comparison/mlp-ellipse-to-star-nystrom-20260903/),
the older full-basis loop had genuinely recovered `k=5` by iteration 50. Its
final Kress geometry had `1.626 mm` centre error, `k=5` components
`(12.055, -0.558) mm`, and `7.467 mm` dense maximum target distance. Because
that run predates the canonical/audit-only state split, it is directional
evidence for basis availability, not validation of the corrected optimizer.
It motivated the then-current one-stage joint default.

The progressive run took `377.523 s`: direct forwards used `232.739 s`
(`225.264 s` in Kress), neural re-distancing `88.257 s`, and geometry/MLP
audits `55.849 s`. Boundary extraction was still absent from modal forwards;
the higher BEM share reflects 1,458 direct forward attempts rather than a
return of the extraction bottleneck.

### Completed joint-`k<=6` diagnostic and the remeshing correction

The matched joint Kress bundle
[`mlp-joint-k6-ellipse-to-star-kress-20260903/`](../results/inverse_solver_comparison/mlp-joint-k6-ellipse-to-star-kress-20260903/)
also did not converge. It accepted 14 updates before the explicit
`representation_limited_stationary` stop. Training loss fell
`1.423241629 -> 0.542048191`; training relative L2 fell
`1.192384 -> 0.738845`, holdout relative L2 fell
`1.053290 -> 0.935345`, and maximum boundary error fell
`42.031 -> 35.114 mm`. Those data improvements are real but still leave a
poor reconstruction. The canonical stored-curve audit found
`k=5 = (-0.420, -4.817) mm` against the target `(12.500, 0) mm`, a
`13.788 mm` vector error.

The decisive failure was the curve parameterization. Repeated
phase-preserving accepted updates drove minimum speed divided by mean speed
from approximately `1` to `0.003` and maximum absolute curvature to about
`3.03e6 1/m`. The solver then accumulated 83 dense-validation rejections.
The MLP was not the acceptance blocker: all 14 re-distance attempts succeeded,
and representation-extraction, representation-drift, and spectral-tail
rejection counts were zero. Final MLP-to-canonical drift was `0.525 mm` and
the response discrepancy was `0.006237`; these miss the stricter convergence
gate but remain below the `1.5 mm` acceptance safety cap. The run explicitly
requested `k=6`, one mode above the 12-angle `k=5` identifiability limit, so it
cannot validate the reconstruction bandwidth either.

Total inverse time was `96.102 s`: direct forwards used `48.895 s`, including
`46.369 s` in Kress and `1.618 s` in curve updates; re-distancing used
`26.810 s`, and dense geometry/MLP audits used `20.132 s`. There were 472
forward attempts, nine infeasible attempts, 83 full-validation rejections,
and no failed MLP fit, extraction, drift, or spectral-tail rejection. The
maximum inverse linear-system residual remained `8.58e-15`. Boundary
extraction therefore remained outside the modal forward loop; the failure was
geometric parameterization, not BEM convergence or a return of the extraction
bottleneck.

That correction retained phase-preserving finite-difference probes and
preliminary line search, but does not promote that selected representation
directly. It rebuilds the same selected modal update with arc-length
redistribution, performs full continuous validation, then runs a fresh BEM
solve and repeats the sufficient-decrease check on the remeshed curve. Only
that coherent curve/response pair may proceed to MLP re-distancing and become
the next canonical state. This addresses the measured collapse without adding
extraction to every probe. Its post-correction long inverse is documented
later in this section.

The same evidence also tightens the default displacement trust radius from
`4.0` to `2.0 mm` (`--maximum-modal-update-mm`). The joint run's first seven
accepted steps all saturated the former cap at `3.975--3.998 mm` before the
parameterization collapsed. Halving the cap is a safety change motivated by
that trajectory, not yet a calibrated convergence optimum.

### Why the modal step is regularized

The dated measurements in this subsection predate the canonical-curve probe
path and are retained as failure evidence for the regularization choices. The
first runs produced a measured pathology worth recording, because nothing in
the acceptance rules forbade it. On both circle targets the contour converged
toward a five-lobed star: the `k=5` radial harmonic grew monotonically to
9-13 mm on a ~52 mm mean radius while the centre and mean radius converged
correctly. The target polyline in those bundles is a true circle, so this was
the iterate, not a plotting artifact.

Four independent choices combined to produce it, and all four are now closed:

- The Levenberg damping was multiplied by `damping_decrease` after every
  accepted cycle and never reset, so twelve accepted cycles took it from 1e-3
  to about 5e-10. The step was effectively undamped Gauss-Newton on a
  rank-deficient normal matrix. `minimum_damping` is now a floor.
- At `ka` of order one the highest column of the modal Jacobian is nearly
  null, and Gauss-Newton inverts its small singular value. Marquardt scaling
  does not help here: it is scale-invariant by construction, so it preserves
  the blow-up instead of pricing it. `curvature_penalty_weight` adds a `k**4`
  Tikhonov ridge, referred to the largest diagonal of the normal matrix, so an
  unresolvable mode is bought at its bending energy rather than for free.
- An oversized step was brought inside the field-update trust region by
  rescaling. Rescaling preserves direction, so a step dominated by the
  invisible mode stayed dominated by it and the resolvable content was divided
  away -- which is also why `maximum_modal_field_update_m` read exactly its cap
  at every iteration and the loss decayed log-linearly without ever
  plateauing. The region is now enforced by raising the damping, which
  shortens the step *and* rotates it toward steepest descent;
  `max_trust_region_solves` first brackets a feasible damping and
  `trust_region_refinement_steps` refines that bracket in log damping. This
  selects the least over-damped feasible direction instead of accepting a
  decade endpoint far inside the trust region; rescaling survives only as the
  bounded-search fallback.
- Acceptance asked for any decrease. A ripple that buys 1e-6 of loss by
  fitting representation error -- the bandwidth-limited Fourier fit, the
  extraction grid, the re-distancing residual -- passed that test forever.
  `armijo_coefficient` now requires the canonical curve response to realize a
  fraction of the decrease its own linear model predicted. Re-distancing no
  longer owns a second data-acceptance solve: its extracted zero set is checked
  for representational fidelity while the already-scored direct curve remains
  the optimization state.

A fifth coupling made convergence unreachable rather than wrong. The inner
re-distancing stopped at its own `eikonal_rms_tolerance` of 0.2 while the outer
loop refused to call anything under 0.15 converged, so every run could only end
at `maximum_iterations`. `AlternatingNeuralInverseConfig` now rejects that
configuration outright. The driver sets the inner tolerance to 0.1 -- under the
outer gate, but not far under, because a wrong ellipse or star needs about
6e-2 to fit at all within the step budget.

Stationarity is deliberately stricter than accepting one fit. Two consecutive
updates must have small realized loss and contour changes, a small
un-backtracked trust-region predicted change, MLP-to-canonical contour drift
and boundary-field residual below the geometry tolerance, and Eikonal RMS
below its gate. The same representation conditions apply when a line search
finds only a negligible trial, so a mismatched MLP cannot be labelled
converged merely because the canonical data step has stalled.

The `k**4` weight and its `1e-4` coefficient are not a guess. The normalized
diagonal of the modal normal matrix, `diag(J^T J) / max`, measured on the first
Jacobian of each target:

| `k` | circle target, `ka = 1.67` | star target, `ka = 5.01` |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | `1.000` | `0.965` |
| 1 | `0.986` | `0.168` |
| 2 | `0.898` | `0.504` |
| 3 | `0.384` | `0.267` |
| 4 | `0.090` | `0.251` |
| 5 | `0.011` | `0.403` |
| 6 | -- | `1.000` |
| 7 | -- | `0.978` |

On the circle the `k=5` column carries about 1% of the strongest mode's
sensitivity, so Gauss-Newton amplifies it by roughly 90 -- that is the ripple,
measured rather than assumed. The prior's threshold there is `1e-4 * 5**4 =
0.0625` against a diagonal of `0.011`, so it dominates by about five; at `k=3`
it is 2% of the diagonal and at `k=2` it is 0.2%, so the informative modes are
untouched.

The star is a different regime, and the same weight handles it. At `ka = 5` the
high modes are the *best*-determined columns, not near-null ones: `k=6` and
`k=7` sit at `1.000` and `0.978`. The prior is then a 13-24% ridge rather than
a suppression, so lobe recovery is not blocked. This is why the mode budget is
reported rather than enforced -- on the star, the direction that manufactures a
spurious ripple on a circle is the one carrying the answer, and only the
measured conditioning tells the two apart.

In the pre-canonical implementation,
`mlp-star-to-star-nystrom-20260903` was the direct test of that claim. Radial
harmonics of the recovered contour against the analytic target, in millimetres:

| mean radius | `50.000` | `50.13` | `50.16` |

The lobe depth is recovered to 2.0% (Kress) and 2.6% (MOD) with every spurious
harmonic under `1.3 mm`, and both solvers stop on descent exhaustion rather

The same `k=5` column that had to be suppressed on the circle carries the
answer here, and the curvature prior priced it correctly in both regimes
without being told which case it was in.

The regularization is not a licence to widen the basis anyway.
[`mlp-ellipse-to-circle-mode5-control-20260903/`](../results/inverse_solver_comparison/mlp-ellipse-to-circle-mode5-control-20260903/)
repeats `mlp-ellipse-to-circle` at `--maximum-mode 5`, above its estimate of 3,
and buys nothing with the extra freedom:

| basis | solver | final training rel. L2 | holdout rel. L2 | max boundary error |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `k <= 3` | Kress | `1.457e-2` | `4.940e-1` | `6.050e-3 m` |
| `k <= 5` | Kress | `1.510e-2` | `6.504e-1` | `1.515e-2 m` |
| `k <= 3` | MOD | `1.444e-2` | `5.089e-1` | `6.152e-3 m` |
| `k <= 5` | MOD | `1.987e-2` | `7.300e-1` | `1.853e-2 m` |

The same data fit costs 2.5-3x the shape error on both solvers, and the wider
run leaves residual `k=1`, `k=6`, `k=7` content of `5.3`, `4.8` and `3.1 mm`
where the capped basis leaves `1.0`, `0.6` and `0.1 mm`. What the
regularization removes is the runaway -- no single unresolvable harmonic grows
monotonically to 20% of the radius any more, and the transient `k=4` excursion
at iteration 30 decays again by iteration 60. Extra basis freedom still buys a
worse reconstruction at equal data fit, which is what an underdetermined
inverse does with freedom it cannot pay for.

The `k**4` ridge prices one proposed increment. The previous local-normal
curve updates could create persistent radial structure above the active basis:
the resolved joint run grew its `k>5` tail from `0.723` to `6.258 mm` RMS and
eventually ceased to be star-shaped. The authoritative radial Fourier state
eliminates that accumulation by construction. Its tail above the global mode
is roundoff-only; during a warm stage, the reported tail also includes higher
coefficients retained for later stages.

Eikonal SGD is intentionally not run inside a forward evaluation. Doing so
would make the parameter-to-residual map path-dependent and invalidate the
finite-difference cache and central Jacobian. Eikonal loss also cannot create
or preserve a desired contour by itself (`F(x,y)=x` already has zero Eikonal
loss and an open zero line). The separate re-distancing stage therefore uses
signed-distance, zero-value, normal-offset, and exterior-box supervision, has a
finite iteration cap, and must meet distance/boundary/Eikonal tolerances. The
MLP is then extracted once with the ordinary Method-B topology and regularity
checks. Its zero set must remain within the representation-drift gate of the
canonical candidate; failure rolls back the MLP and curve together. Cheap and
full validation rebuild identical radial-state nodes, so the selected probe's
BEM response already belongs to the accepted curve; no further solve is needed
for full validation or MLP extraction. The default `1.5 mm` maximum-drift gate is an acceptance safety cap,
not a convergence tolerance. A convergence claim still requires both drift
and the MLP boundary-field residual below the independent `0.2 mm`
geometry-convergence tolerance.
The trainer audits before constructing Adam, but that no-op path uses a
separate 1-micrometre boundary threshold so an ordinary sub-millimetre modal
step is not mistaken for an already represented contour. Stopping and patience
cannot run during the distance-only warm-up; all comparisons use the same full
Eikonal-weighted objective. The lowest full-objective parameter state,
including the input state, is restored before the final audit. Sampling is
fixed across candidates in one outer iteration and advanced between outer
iterations.
The curve-drift check uses symmetric point-to-closed-polygon distance rather
than nearest-node distance, so a harmless periodic phase shift is not counted
as fit motion. Probe curves receive inexpensive node-polygon, bounds, and
regular-jet checks. On the current radial-state path, a line-search candidate
with sufficient data decrease is rebuilt identically for dense validation, so
its probe BEM response remains authoritative. The legacy normal-update path
still arc-length remeshes the selected candidate and repeats BEM acceptance.
The distilled MLP subsequently receives the full SDF-to-Method-B audit. Expected geometry
failures trigger stencil shrinking or backtracking. `max_backtracks` counts
reductions after the un-backtracked trial. Its new default of six evaluates the
full trial and six reductions through `1/64`; the former five-reduction limit
omitted the sixth reduction that passes the measured stage-2 dense-validation
barrier. A full accepted step earns reduced damping; a backtracked accept now
keeps the damping that generated its direction instead of making the next
iteration repeat the reject/escalate search. The failure classifier also
retains the best direct candidate. Stationary motion accompanied by repeated
dense-validation rejection stops as `geometry_limited_stationary`; stationary
motion with no such geometry barrier but an unmet MLP gate stops as
`representation_limited_stationary`. Neither diagnosis claims convergence or
burns the remaining outer budget. Forward-attempt counts
include infeasible trials, and the reported inverse residual maximum includes
every successful finite-difference, rejected, and accepted solve. Rejection
diagnostics separately count full continuous-validation failures, failed MLP
fits, representation-extraction failures, and representation-drift failures;
those events are no longer all mislabeled as infeasible forward evaluations.

Timing is split by ownership. `inverse_forward_seconds` is the complete wall
time for direct curve construction/checks and MOD or Kress objective probes;
`inverse_bem_seconds` and `inverse_curve_update_seconds` expose the backend and
normal-update portions of that total. `inverse_redistance_seconds` covers
neural signed-distance/Eikonal training, while
`inverse_geometry_audit_seconds` covers selected-candidate dense curve
validation and extraction of the MLP representation. Attempt and accumulated
step counts include failed MLP fits as well as accepted ones. Final artifacts
also report canonical-versus-MLP curve drift and response discrepancy, so an
accurate canonical reconstruction cannot hide a poor neural representation.
The live progress record and trajectory CSV additionally carry the accepted
backtrack count, both the raw trust-region and accepted-step predicted relative
decreases, the arc-length-refit RMS and maximum displacement, and speed ratio
before and after refitting. These fields make renewed damping loss or
parameterization collapse observable during a run rather than only in a
postmortem.

from `948.326 s` in the pre-canonical run. Direct inverse forwards consumed
`28.375 s`, of which Kress BEM solves took `26.744 s` and curve updates
`1.007 s`. Neural re-distancing took `174.511 s` and selected-candidate

therefore no longer charged inside every modal forward; re-distancing and

## Star target: recovering lobe depth and phase

The circle cases can only ever exercise a center and a size. The star target
adds the two controls a circular truth cannot identify at all: how deep the
lobes are and where they point.

The truth is the same five-lobe star the forward comparison study already
treats as its reference shape,

```text
center = (0.50, 0.50) m,  r(t) = 0.05 (1 + 0.25 cos(5 t)) m,
```

and the observations come from `nystrom_ref`, the from-scratch Nystrom/Muller
oracle that shares only the problem definition with the two solvers under
test. `solvers/sdf_inverse/targets.py` holds the shape and
`solvers/sdf_inverse/nystrom_oracle.py` the observation seam; a unit test
asserts the shape's parameterization equals `nystrom_ref.star_parameterization`
bit for bit, so the inverse target and the forward study's target are one
curve rather than two transcriptions of one formula.

Independence is not the same as unrelatedness, and the difference matters
here: Kress and the oracle are separate implementations of the same
Nystrom/Muller *formulation*, so a shared formulation error would not be
exposed by this comparison, only a shared implementation error would be. MOD's
compressed-cloud discretisation has no such kinship. The oracle is gated
rather than assumed: the driver solves the exact curve at 512 and 256 nodes
before any inverse runs and requires them to agree to `1e-8`. The checked run
measured `2.880e-10`, with a `2.1e-14` linear-system residual.

The five controlled parameters are the center, the log mean radius, the
amplitude, and the rotation. The initialization is wrong in all five:

```text
center = (0.48, 0.52) m, mean radius = 0.060 m, amplitude = 0.12,
rotation = 0.25 rad
```

Three case-specific choices are worth stating, because each is a measured
constraint rather than a preference:

- **Training band.** For a 0.05 m star in sand the lobe depth and phase are
  nearly invisible below about 1 GHz. A single central finite-difference step
  in the rotation changes the field by `1.2e-5` at 0.25 GHz but `1.9e-2` at
  1.5 GHz, so the star trains at 0.5 and 1.5 GHz and holds out 0.25, 1, and
  2.5 GHz. The five-parameter Jacobian of the normalized residual has
  condition number `32` on that training pair.
- **Fourier bandwidth.** An arc-length Fourier curve represents this star
  slowly. Truncating the exact curve's own spectrum gives a maximum boundary
  error of `9.7e-4 m` at bandwidth 10 and `2.9e-5 m` at bandwidth 48, and
  Method B tracks that floor closely (`1.15e-3` and `4.1e-5 m`). The
  extraction grid and the projected-sample count are not the limiter: at fixed
  bandwidth, refining the grid from `129^2` to `385^2` changes the fitted
  boundary error by less than one part in a thousand. The star therefore uses
  bandwidth 48 and 128 nodes, against the circle's 10 and 64.
- **Bounds.** The amplitude is bounded to `[0.05, 0.35]` and the rotation to
  `+/- 0.60 rad`. The amplitude's lower bound is positive because a circular
  star has no identifiable rotation; the rotation bound is narrower than the
  `2 pi / 5` symmetry period so one shape corresponds to one parameter
  vector. All 32 corners of the bound box extract to a valid single component.

Both branches recovered the center to about `3e-9 m` and passed all 30 gates,
including the two new lobe gates and the oracle self-convergence gate. Kress
was `1.58x` faster end to end and its held-out error was `403x` lower.

The star changes which error dominates, and that is its main result. On the
circle, Kress reached `8.6e-11` training error because a bandwidth-10 curve
represents a circle exactly and only the forward discretisation was left. On
the star, Kress' final training error `5.88e-6` sits at its own
true-boundary floor: it has converged to the accuracy of the shared
bandwidth-48 boundary representation, not to the accuracy of its quadrature.
MOD is still limited by its own discretisation instead — its final holdout
error `1.266e-2` is within half a percent of the `1.260e-2` it produces on the
exact target's fitted boundary. Two consequences follow. The Kress holdout
gate is `1e-3` here rather than the circle's `1e-6`, because the geometry
floor and not the solver sets it. And the wall-clock split moves: with a
high-bandwidth fit, repeated extraction and fitting cost more per objective
evaluation than the Kress solve itself.

Both solvers also absorbed a small part of the geometry error into their
parameters, which is visible in the table: each one's final holdout error is
slightly *below* the error it produces on the exact target's own fitted
boundary. This is the expected behaviour of a fit whose model has a
systematic representation error, and it is the reason the true-boundary
control is computed at all.

## Neural representation: the same cases with a SIREN

The parametric cases fix the shape class by construction.  Replacing the
implicit field with the repository's own SIREN removes that, and the same
driver runs it: `--initial-model siren_circle`, `siren_ellipse`, and
`siren_star` warm start a network onto exactly the wrong shapes their
parametric siblings start from, so only the representation differs.

`SirenImplicitField2D` wraps `neural_sdf.SirenSDF2D` with two adapters.  The
extraction box is mapped onto the network's `[-1, 1]^2` design domain, because
`omega_0` is a frequency in that domain and not in metres, and the output is
rescaled by the same factor, which leaves `|grad F| = 1` identical in both.
`pretrain_implicit_field` then regresses the field onto the first-order
distance `F / ||grad F||` of the analytic shape being imitated, with the
repository's `eikonal_loss` applied throughout.  Warm starting is a
precondition rather than a convenience: a randomly initialized network has no
usable zero contour, which this repository already covers with an
expected-failure regression.

### What the finite-difference budget allows

The inverse costs `2 N + 1` forward solves per Jacobian, so the network size is
set by the optimizer, not by the architecture:

| hidden x layers | weights | solves per Jacobian | warm-start RMS | contour error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 16 x 0 | 65 | 131 | `4.3e-3 m` | `5.8e-3 m` |
| 32 x 0 | 129 | 259 | `2.0e-3 m` | `3.5e-3 m` |
| 16 x 1 | 337 | 675 | `6.0e-4 m` | `1.2e-3 m` |

The checked runs use 129 weights.  A 128-wide SIREN, the architecture's own
default, has 33,537: parameter finite differences would need 67,075 forward
solves per Jacobian, or roughly eleven hours per iteration at this problem's
solve rate.  That is the whole argument for an adjoint stated as a number.

One tuning result is worth recording because it is not obvious.  SIREN's
default `omega_0 = 30` assumes inputs in `[-1, 1]`; applied to a 0.4 m box, a
small network could not fit a smooth cone at all, and every configuration
except one failed contour validation outright.  At `omega_0 = 10` on
normalized coordinates the same networks train without trouble.

### The representation floor

A neural result cannot be read against the parametric thresholds, because its
accuracy is capped by what the network can express.  Each neural run therefore
warm starts a second, identical network directly onto the exact target and
measures it through the same pipeline.  That control is the floor.

For the circle target at 129 weights, the network fitted *to the answer* still
leaves `1.8e-3 m` of boundary error and a `14.8%` held-out field error.  The
parametric circle case reaches `3.6e-12 m` and `3.8e-10` with Kress.  So at
this size the representation is not a contributor to the error budget; it is
the entire budget, and the shape and accuracy gates for neural runs are set
relative to that floor rather than in absolute terms.

### Topology, and a policy the optimizer needed

A general field's zero set can split part way through an otherwise reasonable
step.  It did, immediately: the first Gauss--Newton step over 129 weights
produced a field with four components and aborted the run.

`OrderedSDFGeometryError` already documented that a line search *may* treat
this as an infeasible trial; the optimizer simply never did.
`ParameterFDConfig.infeasible_trial_policy` now names the choice.  The default
`"error"` keeps propagating, which is right for the parametric models, whose
bounds are chosen so that a lost contour means the bounds were wrong.
`"reject"` scores the trial as an infinite objective, which the existing
damping and backtracking logic then rejects with no special case, and a
finite-difference probe with no valid contour degrades to the one-sided
difference that survives, or freezes that column for the iteration.  Both the
rejected-trial count and the largest number of frozen columns are reported.

### Eikonal, and what the runs measure about it

The Eikonal penalty is the only reason the warm-started field is distance-like
at all: it holds `|grad F|` within `0.25` of one on the contour, and that is
gated.  Nothing re-imposes it once the weights start moving, so each run also
records the deviation on the *final* contour.  The drift between those two
numbers is the measurement that says whether a reprojection step between
forward runs would be worth its cost.

## What was repaired in the legacy MOD inverse

The existing `run_ibim_circle_inverse_bscan.py` remains a separate MOD-only
neural/adjoint pipeline. Its audit found four concrete reliability problems:

- the canonical driver passed an unsupported `min_boundary_samples` keyword;
- a CUDA-capable Torch installation selected CuPy assembly without checking
  that CuPy could actually be imported;
- any adjoint shape-gradient exception silently selected a sparse finite-
  difference fallback; and
- the inverse smoke test used one frequency, for which the trapezoidal
  frequency weight was zero and the nominal B-scan objective was degenerate.

The unsupported keyword was removed. Backend resolution now requires both
Torch CUDA availability and an importable CuPy package, otherwise assembly
uses NumPy. The shape-gradient policy now defaults to `error`; finite
differences are available only through the explicit `finite_difference`
policy, and every iteration records the method used. The smoke test now uses
two frequencies and asserts a finite, nonzero objective and dual. These are
hardening fixes, not evidence that the legacy random-SIREN benchmark now has
the accuracy guarantees of the new circle-parameter comparison.

## Scope and limitations

- **Every checked case has zero model error: the initial family contains the
  target exactly.** The star family reproduces the target star at the right
  five parameters; the ellipse family reproduces the circle at equal
  semi-axes; the random-feature family reproduces it at zero output weights.
  The star's lobe count and functional form are given by construction, and its
  rotation bound is narrower than half the symmetry period, so the search
  cannot even reach a wrong lobe basin. These runs therefore measure whether
  the extraction/fit/remesh/forward chain can drive an implicit field onto a
  representable target, and how much forward-solver error biases the recovered
  parameters. They are not evidence about shape discovery, non-uniqueness,
  local minima, or model misspecification, because a bounded low-dimensional
  search containing the exact answer removes most of that difficulty. The
  next credible step for this baseline is a case whose family cannot represent
  the target, which also needs gates written against the best achievable
  approximation rather than against the truth.
- The star result covers a smooth radial graph with a fixed, known lobe count.
  The lobe count is structure, not a control: an integer cannot be recovered
  by continuous finite differences, and a fractional one would not close the
  contour. Recovering topology or lobe count is a different problem.
- The common domain is one smooth, simple, closed, regular component with a
  negative-inside implicit sign. Missing, open, boundary-touching, multiple,
  self-intersecting, or non-regular zero sets fail explicitly; there is no
  component-selection or representation fallback.
- Marching squares can emit adjacent duplicate vertices when a zero contour
  lies exactly on grid nodes. The front end removes only those consecutive
  near-duplicates before polygon validation; non-adjacent repeated geometry
  is still rejected as a genuine degeneracy.
- Because Kress is one of the compared forwards, the shared run currently
  uses an even node count, separated source/receiver points, nonmagnetic media
  (`mur=1`), and lossless media (`sigma=0`). Corners, holes, nested materials,
  and multiple components are outside this baseline.
- The extraction box is `((0.30, 0.30), (0.70, 0.70)) m`, wide enough to
  contain every allowed center/radius trial with at least `0.01 m` clearance.
- Finite differences operate on the complete black-box parameter-to-field
  map, so extraction and remeshing are included. They scale as at least two
  forward evaluations per parameter per Jacobian. The checked models have
  three, four, and seven controls; this is useful for validation, not training
  thousands of SIREN weights.
- A randomly initialized SIREN need not contain exactly one usable closed zero
  contour. Before it can enter this pipeline it needs a topology-valid
  initialization or pretraining contract. Even then, parameter finite
  differences are not a viable optimizer for a full network. A deterministic
  unconstrained random-MLP regression confirms that such a field is rejected
  during contour validation before either BEM forward is called.
- `gpr_bem_kress` remains forward-only. Its retained `A`, `C=[D,-S]`, state,
  and receiver data expose an adjoint seam, but there is no Kress operator
  adjoint, shape derivative, or autograd path. A scalable neural inverse needs
  derivatives of the actual weighted Kress blocks, incident field, receiver
  map, points, normals, speeds, source Jacobians, and diagonal split, together
  with finite-difference gradient acceptance tests.
- Kress remains outside `solver_select`; the new comparison imports both peer
  packages directly. `run_ibim_circle_inverse_bscan.py --solver=mod` still
  names the maintained legacy adjoint path.

The next credible milestone is therefore not to remove the low-dimensional
baseline. It is to use it as the oracle for a Kress discrete-adjoint and
topology-controlled neural coupling, then require both parameter-level and
directional-gradient agreement before scaling the number of SDF parameters.
