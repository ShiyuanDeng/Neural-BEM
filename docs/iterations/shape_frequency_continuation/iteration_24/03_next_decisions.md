# What another iteration would need to establish

2026-09-26. Written while the frozen SC-042/043/044 comparisons are running.
This is an interpretation framework, not another frozen experiment or an
independent review. The final campaign closeout takes precedence over these
conditional decisions.

The useful question is which unresolved mechanism warrants another test.
Changing a cutoff or threshold until a development case wins does not answer
the novelty question.

| Evidence from this campaign | Consequence | Next discriminating test |
|---|---|---|
| A one-off cleanup ties recurrent restrictions after repairing a corrupted start | Keep the simpler repair as an experimental baseline; recurring restriction has not earned its complexity | A prospective, truth-free intervention trigger on full reconstructions, compared with a fixed established filter |
| A permanent state cap helps some shapes but harms true sharp features | Stored-coordinate bandwidth is an inadequate universal regularity criterion | Compare an intrinsic curvature-spectrum constraint and a smooth roll-off, with feature errors and numerical resolution reported separately |
| The atlas fails against either cheap decision rule after diagnostic costs | Reject the frozen rule as a superior controller under this cost model | A new hypothesis about decision value or reuse of already-paid information, frozen and evaluated on untouched cases; do not tune the 10% threshold on these outcomes |
| The atlas wins against both controls with qualified endpoints | It earns a transfer experiment, not a general-method claim | Full reconstructions on new shapes and new noise patterns, with the policy unchanged |
| A lower-loss trial fails field or derivative refinement | The stopping event is a numerical qualification barrier | A separately costed resolution-adaptation comparison; do not relabel it as convergence or unobservability |
| Noisy paths reach discrepancy with residual geometry error | More noiseless optimization may be irrelevant to the observed limitation | New noise draws and independent data channels, then controlled model mismatch; distinguish stability from model bias |
| Identical audits time out under host pressure but pass serially | The original operational failure remains; the endpoint gains separate numerical evidence | Use bounded memory and fresh workers for subsequent studies; report all original and follow-up costs |

## Three distinctions the next proposal must preserve

**Repair and prevention.** SC-042 starts from saved development endpoints,
including the kite artifact. Repairing that known state does not discover
when to intervene in a new reconstruction. In SC-044 the common prefix has
K20: an initial K64 truncation would be a no-op. A deployable cleanup rule
needs a prospective trigger with no truth geometry.

The repository has already encountered a curvature constraint that excludes
the exact star: [iteration 06](../iteration_06/01_results.md). A new intrinsic
constraint must declare its physical scaling and training-case feasibility
audit before transfer testing. Test truth may be used to diagnose bias after
the run, but must not tune that constraint retroactively.

**Diagnostic fidelity and decision value.** A qualified Jacobian and a
coordinate-invariant physical norm can make a prediction mathematically
well defined. They do not establish that acquiring that prediction is worth
more than spending the same work on fitting. SC-043 charges for both coarse
and refined diagnostics. Possible reuse is a future implementation change,
not a retroactive discount on these runs. Its forecasts concern an optimal
linearized step; the ensuing multi-step LM decrease is not a direct
forecast-calibration measurement.

There is also a structural limit to this particular decision problem. Under
the declared reciprocal-batch accounting, adding normal-basis columns does
not add a charged frequency batch: both choices still use the same 19
frequencies and grids. The atlas must earn its overhead through better
trajectories or fewer rejected trials; retaining a smaller M does not itself
save those field evaluations. Changing the frequency set could affect that
cost directly, but SC-043 does not test such a decision. This distinction is
part of interpreting the frozen cost model, not a reason to discount its
diagnostic work after the results arrive.

**Capacity, regularity and information.** Increasing M changes available
normal directions; changing K changes the stored curve and projected tangent
map; changing quadrature changes numerical fidelity. Noise and acquisition
determine which perturbations can be stably inferred. An uncomputed atlas
tail, a small residual, or one failed trial cannot identify all these
mechanisms. A claim of unobservability needs noise-scaled singular information
for the actual acquisition and a qualified perturbation construction.

The closest established filter/resampling and curvature-constrained methods
remain mandatory comparators for a method-level novelty claim; see the
[claims review](02_claims_review.md). No current result licenses claiming
filtering, frequency continuation, or constrained least squares as new.
