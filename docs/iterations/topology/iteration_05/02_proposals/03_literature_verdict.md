# Literature verdict on the stalled topology reconstructions

**The strongest next step is to repair the loss of finite-difference information at the active radius constraint, then address the separate lack of shape degrees of freedom after birth. Neither intervention is established as a complete reconstruction fix.** The evidence does not justify relaxing the radius floor, adding a merge-headroom penalty, or replacing the geometry representation as the next implementation.

Two confidence statements must remain separate. There is **high confidence in the need to correct an invalid derivative model and an insufficient approximation space**. There is only **medium confidence that these corrections will recover the currently failing noncircular scenes**, and insufficient evidence to promise that any proposed change will pass all twelve scenes. If “the fix” means a demonstrated solution of the complete benchmark, no candidate currently meets that standard.

This verdict assesses the repository at `ae10a9c` on `feature/ordered-boundary-nystrom`, with literature checked through 2026-09-12. It supersedes the recommendations in the [preliminary literature pointers](02_literature_context.md) and qualifies several interpretations in the [stall diagnostic](01_stall_diagnostic.md). Existing measurements remain unchanged. This is a written review: no numerical implementation, new inverse run, experiment approval, or controller-default change accompanies it.

**1. Evidence and its limits**

The working tree was clean at the start of this review. The relevant progression is B0 (`345038a`), the twelve-scene benchmark (`116b9b3`), the refined feasibility guard (`8f8a5a4`), its qualification (`2071e5f`), the stall diagnostic (`b31849d`), and the literature hold (`ae10a9c`). The TOP-007 manifest pins the executed numerical source to `8f8a5a4`; subsequent changes through the review starting point concern evidence and documentation. All 155 current source files listed in that manifest match their recorded SHA256 hashes.

The frozen problem is two-dimensional, noiseless, separated, same-material transmission scattering. It uses 24 paired point-source/receiver observations at 0.5 GHz, with 1.5 and 2.5 GHz reserved for evaluation. This is **24 complex observations at the training frequency**, not a full 24-by-24 multistatic response matrix. The exterior/interior relative permittivities are 6/3, with unit relative permeability and zero conductivity. Production/refined boundary resolutions are 64/128 nodes; observation generation uses checked 256/512-node discretizations. These details limit transfer from papers using sound-soft obstacles, plane waves, full multistatic data, three-dimensional Maxwell fields, or structural design.

The guarded arm returns 9/12 runs, compared with 7/12 for the default. Exceptions fall from two to zero, timeouts remain three, and both arms pass five scenes. The following values come from the saved [suite metrics](../../../../../results/validation/topology/TOP-007-20260911-refined-feasibility/suite_metrics.json), rather than rounded narrative summaries. **2. Corrections to the previous causal explanation**

**The 8-mm floor is an optimizer/controller rule, not an intrinsic Kress requirement.** In [radial_topology.py](../../../../../solvers/sdf_inverse/radial_topology.py), `evaluate()` rejects `component_radius_floor(c) < minimum_component_radius_m` before calling the physical objective. The extra-resolution check is separate. The earlier uncaught exception instead came from the multicomponent adapter's 10-mm cross-component clearance requirement. A numerical implementation refusing an objective evaluation does not establish that the physical forward problem is undefined there.

Le Digabel and Wild distinguish constraints that must hold for meaningful simulation output from restrictions that may be violated during optimization. Their taxonomy also depends on how the problem is supplied to the optimizer. Here, the floor behaves as an unrelaxable constraint in the existing wrapper; whether some violations would still allow accurate forward evaluation is a separate question. The taxonomy identifies that distinction and supplies no permission to relax either guard. [4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.07881)

**“Feature radius” currently means a conservative radial certificate.** For a gauge-fixed radial profile,

\[
\rho(\theta)=\rho_0+\sum_{k=2}^{K_r}(a_k\cos k\theta+b_k\sin k\theta),\qquad
c=\rho_0-\sum_{k=2}^{K_r}\sqrt{a_k^2+b_k^2},
\]

the code enforces `c >= 0.008`. This follows directly from `RadialFourierCurveState.minimum_radius_lower_bound_m` in [curve_updates.py](../../../../../solvers/sdf_inverse/curve_updates.py); the gauge-fixed Cartesian implementation computes the corresponding radial-spectrum certificate in [explicit_fourier.py](../../../../../solvers/sdf_inverse/explicit_fourier.py). It is a lower bound on center-to-boundary radius, not a measurement of local neck width, minimum curvature radius, reach, or quadrature error. Its dependence on the chosen center and coefficient magnitudes matters. The Cartesian fallback outside this gauge has different branches, so the simplified formula must not be assumed to describe every possible controller state.

An elementary calculation using the frozen truth shows why these quantities cannot be exchanged. The five-lobed star has `rho0 = 36 mm` and modulation `a = 0.24`. Its radial certificate is `36(1 - 0.24) = 27.36 mm`, comfortably above the floor. At a lobe tip, its curvature radius is

\[
R_{\rm tip}=\frac{\rho_0(1+a)^2}{1+a+a m^2}
=\frac{36(1.24)^2}{1.24+0.24(25)}\;\mathrm{mm}
\approx7.65\;\mathrm{mm}.
\]

This uses the polar-curve curvature formula at a point where `rho' = 0`; it is an analytic consequence of the [scene definition](../../../../../config/topology_scenes_v1.json), not an experimental result. **Replacing the current floor with an 8-mm minimum curvature radius would exclude this benchmark truth.** A replacement geometric regularizer therefore needs its own definition and compatibility evidence.

**The common wrong endpoint does not eliminate initialization or local minima.** Far and empty starts converge to essentially the same wrong shapes, but both subsequently use the same birth construction, merge fitting, optimizer, and constraints. This is evidence for a shared failure mechanism, not a proof that the objective or representation uniquely determines the outcome. Likewise, a 1.4–3.1% residual establishes underfitting; it does not prove that the missing shape information is stably recoverable from these observations.

**The merge holdout failure does not prove a single-frequency impossibility.** Its training residual is small, but neither global optimality nor a sensitivity-based resolution limit has been established. Fixed-frequency uniqueness is possible under suitable, much richer data assumptions; Kress and Rundell explicitly discuss this distinction for sound-soft obstacles. That result does not prove uniqueness for the present penetrable, finite-pair acquisition. It does rule out the blanket statement that one frequency necessarily cannot determine shape. [14](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/pdf/10.1137/S0036139997316598)

**3. Derivative information at the active constraint**

The local code defect is unambiguous. `run_multiradial_fd_inverse.jacobian()` tries both retracted perturbations. If either is infeasible, it inserts a zero residual-Jacobian column. A failed perturbation supplies no evidence that the derivative is zero. The resulting normal equations and `J.T @ residual` then use manufactured information. Moreover, both gradient-tolerance branches can set `converged=True` without checking `frozen_columns`, although other stopping branches do check it. These are code-level observations, not claims that a different optimizer has already recovered the ellipse.

Davar and Grapiglia's §3.4 explicitly constructs feasible forward/backward finite differences for unrelaxable box constraints, choosing the side with adequate step length. Their broader theory concerns convex feasible sets; the unrelaxable extension in that section is specifically for bounds. It supports the stencil principle, not a convergence theorem for this geometry. SciPy's numerical differentiation implementation independently adjusts steps and switches to one-sided formulas near bounds. [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17366v2#S3.SS4) [2](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scipy/scipy/main/scipy/optimize/_numdiff.py)

For an existing gauge direction `d`, the first-order formulas are

\[
Jd\approx\frac{r(R_x(hd))-r(x)}{h},\qquad
Jd\approx\frac{r(x)-r(R_x(-hd))}{h},
\]

using the feasible side, the actual scalar step, and the same retraction `R_x` as the optimizer. When both sides are feasible and comparable, central differences remain appropriate. Independent handling of the two retractions matters: the current shared exception block abandons both if either retraction fails.

**One-sided differences are a correction, not a complete constrained algorithm.** In radial coordinates the floor is a superlevel set of a concave norm expression. At a zero harmonic coefficient, adding either sign can reduce the certificate because the amplitude involves a norm. Both coordinate probes may then fail while a coupled increase in mean radius and harmonic amplitude is feasible. At other active points, a reconstructed LM direction can point out of the feasible region even with an accurate Jacobian. Backtracking that direction need not discover an inward or tangential alternative.

Hough and Roberts construct residual models from feasible interpolation points and use constrained criticality plus model-accuracy checks. The relevant lesson is to preserve a locally informative model over feasible displacements. Their assumptions include a closed convex set with nonempty interior and a cheap projection; the complete geometry/gauge/clearance domain here has not been shown to satisfy them. Their method is consequently a design reference, not an immediately interchangeable solver with inherited guarantees. [3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.05443)

The earlier suggestion to “project each FD direction onto the active tangent” is incomplete. Independently projected directions can collapse or become linearly dependent; their residual differences then describe the projected directions, not the original columns. Also, an inequality allows inward directions. Treating its active boundary as an equality can remove precisely the escape direction needed. Any future constrained-step method must account for the actual sampled displacement matrix and its conditioning, and distinguish inequality feasibility from the already-existing linear gauge restriction.

For the ideal radial certificate alone, an exact norm-constrained local subproblem is conceivable. The complete optimizer additionally includes retraction, geometry validation, component separation and discretization requirements. No equivalence between that simplified subproblem and the full implementation has been established. This is why full feasible-direction or constrained trust-region machinery ranks below the direct stencil correction.

**4. Shape capacity after birth**

The saved `far-two-stars` components both have Cartesian maximum mode 1. The current polar-angle gauge admits only three independent directions at Cartesian `K <= 2`: translation in x/y and uniform radius. A free Cartesian mode-1 ellipse is therefore not an available update in this gauge. The absence of star deformation is a provable approximation-space limitation, even though it is not proven that these particular circles are globally optimal within their restricted space.

The exact relation documented in `polar_angle_gauge_tangent_basis` is radial bandwidth `K_r = K - 1`. Padding a Cartesian circle from `K=1` to `K=2` would still leave it circular; genuine shape directions first appear at `K=3`. A radial five/seven-lobe harmonic needs Cartesian bandwidth at least six/eight. Those facts are useful for evaluating capacity, but **the truth's lobe count must not select bandwidth inside the reconstruction algorithm**. A generic predeclared continuation rule must apply to every scene.

Borges and Greengard relate update bandwidth to frequency and illumination: insufficient bandwidth suppresses detail; excess permits instability. Their sound-soft, far-field, multi-frequency setting supports controlled capacity, without establishing a mode schedule for this acquisition. [10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.5436)

The appropriate limited recommendation is to permit surviving circular components to enter nested richer spaces while preserving their initial geometry. The constructor already supports exact zero padding. Whether added directions explain the training residual must be assessed through trustworthy sensitivities and actual decreases. Promotion alone cannot fix the mode-9 ellipse-star component, and a blanket jump to a large bandwidth would increase both ill-conditioning and FD cost.

There is also a physical reason to resist unlimited promotion. Using the saved exterior material, the wavelength at 0.5 GHz is approximately `c0 / (f sqrt(6)) = 245 mm`; for a 36-mm mean radius, `k rho0` is about 0.92. These are dimensional calculations, not a resolution theorem. They make weak sensitivity to fine harmonics plausible and motivate checking observable directions rather than assuming every added mode can be identified from 24 pairs.

**5. What the closely related scattering literature does and does not support**

Carpio, Dimiduk, Le Louër and Rapún provide particularly relevant prior art: an electromagnetic inverse algorithm combining regularized Gauss–Newton shape correction with topological updates that create, merge and remove components when fitting stagnates. Their method does not require the true number of components. They use three-dimensional Maxwell holography, spherical-harmonic components, analytical derivatives and a noise-based stopping rule. The architectural overlap supports retaining the topology/shape split; their thresholds and formulas cannot be transplanted directly into the present scalar paired-data problem. [9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.12202)

Borges, Rachh and Greengard study homogeneous **penetrable** two-dimensional scattering, making their 2023 paper closer to the physical setting than the sound-soft examples. In §4.4, their single-boundary model approximates multiple scatterers with thin connecting bridges. Their comparisons also show that volume reconstruction is more robust for some complex cavities, whereas boundary reconstruction fares better with limited measurements. Thus bridge artifacts have close published analogues, and volume methods are not uniformly preferable. Their multiple-object experiment uses a single curve and much richer frequency/angular data, so it does not diagnose our incorrect merge or validate a particular replacement. [12](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.11607v1)

Borges and Rachh's §6 is valuable contrary evidence. In their impedance-scattering study, an optimization variant achieves a lower residual at some lower frequencies yet produces a worse final multi-frequency reconstruction. They leave the interaction between optimizer, stopping and continuation unresolved. Improving the current residual is necessary to resolve underfitting, but must not be equated with improving geometry or holdout prediction. [11](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.13489v1#S6)

Burger, Hackl and Ring show why adding topological information can help level-set reconstruction escape failures of shape-only evolution. This supports hybrid topology mechanisms in general; it does not show that rewriting the present implementation as a level set would remove its derivative, capacity, data, or cost limitations. [15](https://ww3.math.ucla.edu/camreport/cam03-46.pdf)

**6. Length scale, headroom and relaxation**

Zhou, Lazarov, Wang and Sigmund formulate differentiable geometric constraints on filtered density fields. Their §3.1.2 reports premature local minima when the constraints are imposed from the initial grey design; §3.2.3 and the conclusion recommend introducing them after a suitable initial topology develops. They also discuss numerical relaxation and parameter tuning. This is meaningful evidence that length-scale restrictions can interact badly with topology evolution, including in formulations that are smooth. It contradicts the earlier suggestion that smooth filtering straightforwardly explains successful convergence while a hard floor alone explains failure. The paper does not prescribe an 8-mm radial-certificate schedule for BIE inversion. [6](https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/files/128050147/Minimum_length_scale_in_topology_optimization_by_geometric_constraints.pdf)

Guest, Prévost and Belytschko's projection method is another distinct mechanism: nodal design variables map to element volume fractions through a length-scale-dependent projection. Its publisher abstract describes minimum-compliance examples without additional constraints or penalties. It is contextual evidence for representation-based length control, not validation of a penalty on our existing boundary coefficients. [7](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.1064)

The local history argues for caution in both directions. The merge certificate falls from approximately 12.09 mm before the event to 8.80 mm after fitting, then to 8.00 mm during refinement. That makes a poorly refinable merge plausible. But the implementation notes also record a failed attempt at a tighter radial bound that allowed an approximately 10-micrometre polar radius and subsequently unusable probes. Neither “keep any hard floor forever” nor “relax it now” follows from these observations.

Adding a headroom threshold would introduce a new prior and could reject an otherwise useful topology change. A certificate margin is not a prediction of feasible objective decrease, and an optimizer can consume any fixed margin later. The stronger question is whether the candidate admits accurate feasible sensitivities and useful refinement under the existing constraints. Correcting the derivative model is prerequisite to answering that question fairly.

Padidar, Larson and Wild study smooth domain warping for unrelaxable bounds. They explicitly identify the danger that the transformed gradient becomes small near a boundary without corresponding stationarity in the original variables, and develop adaptive machinery to address it. A sigmoid/softplus reparameterization is therefore not a free cure for stagnation. Their box-domain analysis does not validate a simple warp for the coupled radial certificate plus separation constraints. [5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.09627)

**7. Ranked candidate fixes**

Ranks express confidence in a useful correction of the **currently evidenced failures within the present project**, accounting for transfer assumptions and intervention size. They are ordinal judgments, not measured probabilities. “High” means a code-level necessity plus directly relevant numerical practice; “medium” means a credible mechanism with an unresolved local link; “low” means mostly analogy or substantial untested changes. Several rows have high confidence in a general method and much less in its effect here.

| Rank | Candidate | Confidence in the stated mechanism | Confidence in recovering the affected v1 failures | Decision |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Preserve feasible FD information; distinguish derivative failure from convergence | **High**: failed probes are not zero derivatives; valid one-sided information can be retained | **Medium** for escaping the ellipse-star stall; low for fixing the entire suite alone | **Accept the narrow correction**; preserve floors and event policy |
| 2 | Controlled bandwidth enrichment of surviving circle components | **High**: the present space cannot express the missing shapes | **Medium** for star recovery; observability and regularization remain unresolved | **Accept capacity correction in a separate comparison** |
| 3 | Feasible-direction/constrained local steps after restoring a trustworthy model | **Medium** for this complete geometry domain; sound constrained-optimization rationale | **Medium, conditional** on evidence that the corrected LM direction remains blocked | **Defer**; do not treat tangent projection as a complete implementation |
| 4 | Multi-frequency continuation with compatible shape complexity and acquisition | **High** as a general inverse-scattering strategy | **Medium** for improved prediction; not proof that the merge failure is exclusively information-limited | **Defer to a separately defined acquisition comparison** |
| 5 | Analytical shape derivatives or differentiated BIE solves | **High** that this derivative route exists; **medium** for reliable transfer into this code | **Low–medium** for timeouts; no dominant-cost diagnosis or speedup measured | **Defer to the boundary–BIE track** |
| 6 | Floor-aware merge fitting or a candidate-headroom acceptance rule | **Medium** that it changes the problematic handoff; **low** that margin predicts success | **Low–medium**; one shared failure trajectory, no controlled comparison | **Defer** until refinement is trustworthy |
| 7 | Lower/relax the floor, replace it with a penalty, or use a smooth warp | **Low** for this implementation; the relevant restrictions and risks differ | **Low**; can trade stagnation for degenerate or biased geometries | **Reject as the next fix** |
| 8 | Replace LM with a generic constrained derivative-free solver | **Medium** that suitable method classes handle unavailable evaluations | **Low** within the current solve budget and geometry domain | **Defer**; no solver swap justified by name alone |
| 9 | Replace explicit boundaries with a volume/level-set representation | **Medium** for difficult topology in the literature | **Low** as the immediate fix for these known local defects | **Reject as the next fix**; retain as a longer-term alternative |
| 10 | More candidate tuning, more events, restarts, or larger iteration/time budgets | **Low** as a common explanation of the present failures | **Low** without a discriminating mechanism | **Do not pursue now** |

Rank 5 has genuine physical precedent: Hohage and Le Louër derive boundary-to-field derivatives and adjoints for dielectric scattering and use regularized Gauss–Newton reconstruction. Their formulation is three-dimensional, smooth and spectral; it neither supplies our implementation nor removes geometric constraints. Also, a scalar adjoint gradient does not by itself replace the complete residual Jacobian used by LM. [13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.10830)

For rank 8, Audet and Dennis's MADS framework supports nonsmooth constrained derivative-free search through richer polling directions. Its convergence conclusions involve assumptions and limiting directional coverage, not a guarantee under a fixed 600-second budget. This is a credible fallback class if derivative construction proves impractical, not evidence that it will outperform a repaired least-squares method here. [16](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/040603371)

The refined feasibility guard is already a qualified reliability improvement, so it is not ranked as a new shape fix. Future interventions should be compared against guarded G to prevent known exceptions from obscuring the mechanism; A remains the historical default reference. Adopting G as the default is still a separate change.

**8. Written next steps supported strongly enough to recommend**

Only the first two corrective mechanisms above warrant concrete next-step proposals now. The stages below are recommendations for a later implementation contract, not authorization to run them. Their purpose is to distinguish a corrected numerical mechanism from a successful inverse reconstruction before spending another full development cycle.

**First: repair feasible derivative estimation and its stopping semantics while leaving the feasible set unchanged.** Keep central differences when valid; independently attempt the feasible side when its counterpart fails; use a bounded step adjustment when necessary. Mark unresolved derivatives as unresolved. A small gradient assembled from missing columns must not be presented as certified convergence. Preserve the current gauge, 8-mm certificate, production/refined admissibility, data, mode counts, topology candidates, margins and default settings.

The first validation should use the saved final guarded `far-ellipse-star` state and an interior control, not a new long topology search. For each gauge direction, record both feasibility verdicts, actual perturbation size, stencil chosen, model displacement and derivative estimate. Check a predeclared three-size stencil sequence against an independently formed feasible directional difference; reducing `h` indefinitely is not acceptable evidence. This is a model-validity check, not a rank or uniqueness certificate. Analytical toy residuals at a boundary can separately expose sign, denominator and stopping errors without any BIE solve.

If consistent feasible derivatives are obtained, one bounded fixed-topology continuation from that saved state is sufficient for the first causal check. Use no more than the existing 22 fixed iterations and the existing 600-second per-run ceiling; also declare a solve cap before execution. Require a genuine objective decrease at both resolutions, using the existing margin convention, and record whether the original blocked component actually moves. A reported change from `infeasible_jacobian` to another stop reason alone is a failure of the hypothesis test.

**Stop this line if** no stable feasible stencil is available within the declared sequence, or a verified local model yields no useful feasible decrease. In the first case, the derivative construction remains unresolved; in the second, one-sided information was insufficient. Neither outcome justifies silently weakening the floor. Rank 3 then needs a separately reviewed case based on the observed failed directions. If training improves while geometry or holdout deteriorates, report optimization improvement only.

**Second: remove the circle-only restriction through controlled, geometry-preserving enrichment.** Keep this separate from the derivative correction so that their effects can be identified. Use the saved `far-two-stars` circular state as the mechanism diagnostic and a circular scene as the control. Specify a generic nested bandwidth ladder, a cap, and a promotion rule before execution. The ladder must expose actual gauge directions, rather than merely increasing stored Cartesian `K` from 1 to 2. Do not use the truth count, lobe count, locations or held-out frequencies to select modes.

At each permitted stage, verify that zero padding preserves the geometry and objective before refinement. Assess added residual directions in a consistent physical scaling: both their sensitivity relative to numerical error and the residual reduction available beyond the existing columns matter. Retain enrichment only under a predeclared training-based rule and unchanged refined feasibility/margin checks. If the new directions are numerically unobservable or consume the bounded effort without meaningful improvement, stop increasing bandwidth. This would narrow the problem toward data, conditioning or regularization rather than justify still more modes.

A later full performance claim must compare the candidate with G on **all twelve unchanged v1 scenes**, using the saved observations, original gates and original per-scene limits. Report each gate, exceptions, timeouts, modes, derivative failures and BIE solve counts; selected frozen-state checks are diagnostics only. A mode-policy change must have a separately named arm/comparison, as the benchmark requires. Keep any successful partial correction even if it does not increase the pass count, but describe its achieved scope accurately.

These recommendations have high confidence only in their narrow outcomes: retaining valid derivative information and making noncircular shapes representable. Whether they produce additional benchmark passes remains the falsifiable question. No literature source establishes a numerical success threshold or a guaranteed recovery rate for this code.

**9. Conditions for reconsidering the deferred ideas**

For the **merge holdout failure**, first establish whether the low-bandwidth, fixed-acquisition solution remains improvable under a trustworthy derivative and adequate representation. A small training residual can coexist with weakly determined boundary directions, early stopping, discretization effects or approximation bias. Use a valid sensitivity/conditioning assessment before calling the acquisition the sole cause. Multi-frequency reconstruction would change the question; frequencies used in fitting would cease to be holdout data, requiring a new untouched evaluation set. The large gaps from 0.5 to 1.5 to 2.5 GHz also do not inherit the small-frequency-step justification of recursive linearization. [10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.5436)

For **timeouts**, the saved checkpoints are poor reconstructions, not merely accurate answers delivered late. Additional bandwidth could increase cost. Stage-level solve accounting and timing must distinguish Jacobian assembly, repeated candidate polishing, geometry/gauge work and factorization before choosing analytic derivatives, caching, screening or allocation changes. No specific performance fix is supported confidently enough yet; increasing the time limit would change the benchmark without resolving that uncertainty.

For **floor reformulation or merge headroom**, require evidence that a trustworthy constrained local method is still prevented from reaching an admissible useful shape. A replacement must preserve the intended protection and be compatible with the frozen truths. Certificate margin, curvature, self-separation, center choice and numerical resolution are different measurements. Do not exchange them under a common “feature size” label.

For **representation replacement**, demand a failure that cannot reasonably be explained by the already observed missing directions, constraints or acquisition. The topology architecture has close prior art, while published boundary/volume comparisons reveal tradeoffs. There is currently no evidence-based reason to restart this effort around an MLP, level set or volume solver.

**10. Source record and applicability**

Primary research, author manuscripts and official software sources underpin the recommendations. Full-text access here means the relevant methods, assumptions and discussion sections were inspected; it does not imply every proof was independently verified. Publisher-abstract-only material is identified and is not used to recommend implementation. Search-result summaries and citation counts are not treated as evidence of effectiveness.

The review covers feasible numerical differentiation, constrained least-squares models and stopping, geometric regularization, hybrid topology/shape inversion, bandwidth and frequency continuation, dielectric shape derivatives, and boundary/volume alternatives. It is a targeted literature assessment, not a systematic exhaustive search or a novelty certification. The exact conjunction of this radial certificate, gauge and frozen-column logic was not established as a published case; close analogues are sufficient to reject an unsupported novelty claim.
